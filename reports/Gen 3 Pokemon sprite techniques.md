# Gen 3 sprites trade detail for bold, ramped, selectively outlined shapes

Generation 3 Pokemon sprites look the way they do because a hard hardware budget (a **64x64 canvas, one 16-entry palette per species with index 0 transparent, so 15 visible colors, each a 15-bit BGR555 value**) was spent on a small set of repeatable decisions. The silhouette is bold and simplified. The outline is a 1px line in a shared **near-black (16,16,16), not pure black**, and it gives way to a dark tint of the neighboring fill on lit, pale or flame-like edges. Each material gets a 3-5 tone ramp with big lightness steps and a top-left light. Interiors are separated mostly by color steps, with a dark line added only where two similar parts overlap. Features are tiny (eyes about 2x2 to 3x3 with one white shine pixel, mouths a 1px line), and pure white is saved for those few highlight pixels. Evidence is uneven. The hardware facts come from the pret/pokeemerald decompilation and are firm. The style findings are **measurements from small, hand-picked samples (8 to 16 Pokemon, plus one trainer sprite)** and visual reading, and the notes found no sourced account of the artists or their workflow. Three results contradict common belief: dithering is used sparingly, the outline is (16,16,16), and a "zero orphan pixels" rule would reject the reference May sprite (13.8% orphans). Every number below carries the notes' evidence tag: measured, sourced, visual, or unverified. Readability metrics are proposals unless a value is shown measured on the May sprite.

## The hardware budget: 64x64, 15 colors and 4 bits per pixel

All Gen 3 front and back pics are 64x64 (8x8 tiles). A per-species table records where the drawn pixels sit: `.size` is the drawn area (multiples of 8) and `.y_offset` is the gap from the drawn area to the canvas bottom ([pokeemerald front_pic_coordinates.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pret/pokeemerald/master/src/data/pokemon_graphics/front_pic_coordinates.h)). Sprites are therefore bottom-anchored per species rather than on a universal ground line. Venusaur is 64x64 with offset 3, Charizard offset 1, Blastoise offset 0, Charmander 40x40 with offset 12, and Caterpie 32x40 with offset 16 (sourced). Bulbapedia, via a search snippet, gives 64x64 for Gen III against 56x56 for Gen I/II ([Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_sprites)). A secondary Bulbapedia note puts Gen IV at 80x80 (sourced, secondary).

Color is BGR555: 5 bits per channel, 32 levels each, bit 15 unused. OBJ palette RAM holds 16 palettes of 16 colors, and color 0 of each palette is transparent ([GBATEK](https://problemkaputt.de/gbatek.htm)). Each species has exactly one `normal.pal` and one `shiny.pal`. Front and back share the palette, and shiny is the same indexed pixels with different color values ([pokemon.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pret/pokeemerald/master/src/data/graphics/pokemon.h)). The notes measured that the shiny Bulbasaur palette changes only the body greens (indices 2-4 and 11-14) and leaves the red tongue and accents unchanged (measured). So regions that must recolor need their own palette indices. Graphics are 4bpp tiles (32 bytes per 8x8 tile), LZ77-compressed to `.4bpp.lz` by the decomp's gbagfx tool from 4-bit indexed PNGs plus JASC-PAL files (sourced; measured that the PNGs are bit depth 4, color type 3). The animated front is `anim_front.png`, 64x128, two 64x64 frames stacked (measured, 3 species). The party icon is 32x64 and uses one of three shared icon palettes ([decomp wiki](https://code.heni.lol/decomp/emerald/wiki/How-to-add-a-new-Pok%C3%A9mon-species)).

The notes record one discrepancy about the color count. The decomp wiki says to "limit yourself to 14 colors," but measured official files use up to 15 visible colors. Charizard's front uses all 16 indices, Bulbasaur's uses 14 visible, and Mew's uses 9 (measured). The hardware limit is 15 plus transparent, and 14 is conservative advice. In the 16-sprite PokeAPI sample, opaque color counts ran from 10 to 15 (measured). Decomp .pal values are 5-bit colors expanded to 8-bit as `c5*255/31` (for example 25 becomes 205). Quantizing to 5 bits per channel therefore reproduces the look. Index conventions are non-binding: white tends to sit at 1 and the outline near the middle or end, but this was seen in only 3 species (measured), and index 15 is a real color in Charizard. Index 0 holds an arbitrary placeholder in the .pal files (for example 205,205,172) that is never displayed.

## Shape language: a chunky silhouette, a mostly selective outline, sparing interior lines

Silhouette claims in the notes are visual or unverified, since no text source or developer statement exists. Opaque area ranges from 502 px (Pichu, 12% of the canvas) to 2643 px (Swampert, 65%), so large designs are crammed into the canvas and small ones sit smaller (measured, 15 sprites). Visually, key features are exaggerated (Pikachu's and Pichu's ears, Swampert's head fins, Charizard's wing panels), small details are merged (fingers and toes become 1-3 nubs, Gengar's spikes become a few sawtooth triangles), and poses are tilted for readability rather than copied straight (visual). Protrusion min-width and gap-filling rules in the notes (at least 2px wide at the base, at least 3px long, fill 1px gaps) are inferences, not observations.

The outline findings are the most developed and overturn a common assumption. Across 16 Emerald sprites the shared outline color is **RGB (16,16,16)**, and it is the darkest color in 15 of 16 sprites. The exception is Gyarados, which has a (0,0,0) discrepancy the notes did not explain (measured). The pret palettes confirm #101010 in all 10 sampled .pal files (measured). The 16 likely comes from 5-bit quantization of a value near 2/31, but that reasoning is unverified. Near-black covers anywhere from **21% to 75%** of the silhouette edge, defined as opaque pixels with a transparent 4-neighbor. Pichu is highest at 0.75, then Marshtomp 0.74, Sceptile 0.64, Swampert 0.59, Pikachu 0.55, Gardevoir 0.47, Charizard 0.36, Bulbasaur 0.34, Gyarados 0.30 and Mudkip lowest at 0.21 (measured). The pattern is that small, compact yellow designs are mostly black outlined while large or colorful ones are heavily selective. Another measurement from palette pixels puts the (16,16,16) share of all opaque pixels at 5-18% and its share on the background edge at 46-78% (measured, 8 species). The remainder of those pixels is inner lines such as eyes, mouths and limb separations.

The rest of the edge uses selective outlining ("selout"): a dark, saturated step of the adjacent fill's own ramp. Charizard's most common edge color is dark orange-brown (148,49,0) at 44% against near-black at 36%, with black reserved for mouth, claws and feet. Mudkip's is blue-violet (74,74,131) at 28%, and Gengar's RS/E sprite is dark purple (74,41,74) 40% against black 38% (all measured). The same Gengar in FRLG flips to black 61% and purple 26%, so FRLG and RS/E differ in outline policy for at least that sprite (measured, one sprite). Selout colors share the fill's hue and are among the darkest two entries of its ramp, and they are often reused as shadow fill, which saves palette slots (measured plus visual; reuse seen in one sprite). The darkest ramp tone is still not the outline: Pikachu's darkest yellow (#835200, luma 86) is far lighter than the outline (luma 16), as is Charmander's (67 against 16) (measured). Outlines are omitted or lightened where a part is light or glowing. Light-facing boundary pixels with luma over 150 are 13% of the edge for Blastoise and Elekid and 10% for Mudkip. Gardevoir's white dress takes a blue-gray (131,148,180) edge, and Charizard's and Blaziken's flames have no black outline (measured and visual). The notes' lines are 1px thick, with very few doubled L-corners (0-7% of the edge, median about 1%) (measured with a crude metric). Diagonals follow regular 1:1, 2:1 and 3:1 steps (visual only; run lengths were not measured).

Interior separation is by color step first and line second, but this is visual from 3-4 sprites. A 1px line in near-black or the darkest ramp step of the part behind appears where a limb overlaps a body of similar value (Charizard arms, Gardevoir's arm against dress). A color boundary alone is used where values already differ (white belly against orange body). The notes' inferred rule is to insert an interior line when adjacent part fills differ by less than about one ramp step, and none when they differ by two or more. This is an inference.

Features are built from tiny blocks. Pikachu's eye is a 3-wide by 4-tall near-black block with one white (255,255,255) shine pixel at upper-left and one mid-gray (106,106,106) pixel rounding a corner (measured crop). Its mouth is a 1px near-black line with a roughly 3x2 red tongue, and its cheeks are 3x3 to 4x4 red blobs with one light and one dark pixel (measured plus visual). Other eyes are 2-3px colored irises with dark contour and a light pixel (visual). Teeth and claws are 1-2px white or pale blocks against dark gaps (visual). Stripes are short 1px dashes in the darkest brown (Pikachu's back) or 2-3px colored bands with one dark edge pixel (Swampert's arm bands) (visual). No source gives minimum feature sizes. Empirically the smallest deliberate feature is 1x1 and most readable features are at least 2x2.

Texture and dithering contradict the common belief that Gen 3 does not dither. Counting 2x2 alternating checkerboard windows in Emerald sprites gives Venusaur 92, Charizard 87, Swampert 59, Gyarados 47, Sceptile 36, Gardevoir 34, down to Pikachu 6, Elekid 5 and Pichu 4 (measured). Dither sits on the boundary of two adjacent ramp steps, 1-2 rows wide, in wing membranes, large bodies and white-dress highlights (visual). Windows overlap, so true coverage is lower than the counts suggest, and the true dithered-pixel percentage was never computed (caveat). Fur uses jagged edges plus a selout rim rather than dither. Flames use 3-4 step tapering ramps. Metal, stone and gem textures were not sampled.

## Color: flat ramps, hue-shifted shadows, top-left light and scarce white

The notes found a budget pattern in 8 palettes: one outline slot, then 2-4 material ramps of 3-5 tones, a few flat accents, one white and one gray. Pikachu uses a 5-tone yellow ramp, a 3-tone red, black, gray and white for 11 colors. Treecko uses 15 colors across green, leaf-green, red and yellow ramps (measured, [pret palettes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pret/pokeemerald/master/graphics/pokemon/pikachu/normal.pal)). Evolution lines and some species share identical ramps (Charmander and Charizard share five hexes, and Bulbasaur and Treecko share a leaf-green ramp), suggesting reusable ramp templates, though this is inferred from hex equality only (measured). Pixel share per tone puts the form on the base and first shade: Pikachu's yellow is highlight 3.7%, base 35.8%, first shade 28.7%, second shade 5.2%, darkest 7.7% (measured). The suggested split of outline 1, primary ramp 4-5, secondary 3-4, tertiary 3 and spare 2-3 is an inference from 8 species.

Light comes from the upper left in all sampled sprites. Light-minus-shade centroid offsets (normalized by bounding box) were x from -0.08 to -0.22 and y from +0.03 to -0.30 (Pikachu (-0.14,-0.10), Charmander (-0.22,-0.14), Snorlax (-0.08,-0.30), Gardevoir (-0.10,+0.03)) (measured, but the light and shade indices were hand-picked, so the method is crude). The darkest ramp tones sit on silhouette edges: 50-63% of the darkest tones touch the background, and highlight and base tones sit at 0-5% (measured). Shading is flat bands, not gradients. The notes infer a normal-based model with light near (-0.5,-0.5,0.7) and the darkest tone forced onto right- and down-facing edges. That is an inference, and the weaker vertical component in Gardevoir and Charizard suggests treating y as secondary.

Hue shifts are material-dependent rather than universal. Pikachu's yellow goes H 60, 56, 48, 42, 38 with saturation at 100 for four of five tones. Charmander's orange goes H 38, 26, 16, 20. Bulbasaur's teal moves toward cyan, H 157, 154, 164, 180, and Gardevoir's green hair goes H 96, 120, 120, 140 (measured). Warm colors move toward red, greens and blues toward cyan-blue, and the darkest steps gain saturation. Bulbasaur's red (fixed H 3), Snorlax's blue-gray (fixed H 240) and Mudkip's blue (stable for three steps, then indigo, desaturated) hold or break the pattern, so hue shift must be a per-material parameter that can be zero (measured). Lightness steps between adjacent tones are typically **25-45 luma** on 0-255, largest between base and first shadow. Four-tone luma targets of about 200-240, 160-190, 105-130 and 60-85 reproduce the observed ramps, with the outline at 16 (measured summary plus inference). Highlights shift less than shadows, drifting toward yellow or white with 10-20 points less saturation (inference from Pikachu's #ffff7b).

Whites follow a strict economy. Pure #ffffff is **0.2-1.9% of pixels** (Pikachu 0.2, Charmander 1.3, Bulbasaur 1.9, Snorlax 1.5, Charizard 1.0), spent on eye shine, teeth and claws. Large white surfaces are a cool off-white ramp: Gardevoir's body is #eeeeff (27% of pixels), #c5cdde, #a4b4cd and #8394b4, with saturation 7-27 and hue about 215-240. Snorlax's cream shifts toward olive in shadow instead of gray (measured). The saturated mid-tones fit the common story that GBA developers exaggerated color to offset a washed-out LCD ([near.sh](https://archive.ares-emu.net/near.sh/articles/video/color-emulation.html)). The notes could not tie any Pokemon palette to that story, and the saturation equally fits a stylistic choice. They conclude that outputs viewed on an sRGB screen need no extra saturation boost.

## From official art to 64x64: almost nothing is sourced

The notes found no citable source naming the Gen 3 sprite artists or documenting their workflow. The indirect evidence is that Red/Green sprites were drawn first and Sugimori's watercolors came later ([Bulbapedia via snippet](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ken_Sugimori); a fan translation of Game Freak material says the same, single source, not official ([Lava Cut](https://lavacutcontent.com/sugimori-hundreds-pokemon-cut/))). Game Freak kept pixel art through Gen 4 ([Bulbanews](https://bulbanews.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pokémon_sprites,_art_evolve_over_the_years)). Smogon describes the Gen 3-5 era as digital with color "no longer restricted" and designs cuter and simpler, which says nothing about workflow ([Smogon](https://www.smogon.com/articles/pokemon-art-through-ages)). The belief that Gen 3 sprites were hand-redrawn from the art rather than auto-downscaled is an inference from hardware limits and the Gen 1 precedent, and the same holds for the fan claim that they simplify and reposition limbs (unverified). Community tutorials agree on practice: work on a 64x64 canvas, index to 16 colors, use black for the outline and darkest shade, and reduce colors before adding detail ([PokeCommunity](https://www.pokecommunity.com/threads/128032), [Pokebip](https://www.pokebip.com/page/fanarts/tutos/pixel-art/basiquessprite)). None of these gives numbers. For the algorithm, the notes borrow from academic work: Gerstner et al. jointly optimize palette and pixel assignment ([Princeton PIXL](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gerstner_2012_PIA/index.php)), and Inglis and Kaplan rasterize line art by pixel-artist conventions ([EG DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/3468d785-2fb6-498f-8947-e1fc587f6675)). Neither is Pokemon-specific, and nothing dedicated to Gen 3 style was found.

## Gen 2, Gen 4 and trainers: what differs

Generation comparisons are thin. Sourced: Johto sprites were limited to two main colors each, Gen 3 lifted that limit, and Gen 4 added many details despite simplified forms ([Smogon](https://www.smogon.com/articles/pokemon-art-through-ages)). Fan opinion calls Gen 2 painterly with beady eyes, Gen 3 full-color and toy-like, and Gen 4 the cleanest ([PokeCommunity](https://www.pokecommunity.com/threads/which-old-gen-had-the-best-series-of-sprites.536419/)). Measured on only n=3-4 Platinum sprites at 80x80, selective outlining continues in Gen 4: Charizard edge black (0,0,0) 204 pixels against brown 201, and Pikachu black 98 against brown 39. Gen 4 may use pure (0,0,0) where Gen 3 uses (16,16,16). Gen 2 Crystal was too limited (4 colors on a white box) to compare. The remaining Gen 2 and Gen 4 style claims (4-color GBC dithering, softer Gen 4 gradients, more dither in Gen 4) are unverified. FRLG versus RSE was not systematically compared. The notes measured one Gengar difference, and an FRLG-keeps-RSE-sprites claim was garbled and is unverified.

Trainer front sprites share the Pokemon format: 64x64, 16 colors, 4bpp, LZ77 ([Data Crystal](https://datacrystal.tcrf.net/wiki/Pokémon_3rd_Generation)). Back sprites are stacked 64x64 frames (Brendan's measured file is 64x256), default 4 frames, sharing the front palette ([decomp wiki](https://code.heni.lol/decomp/emerald/wiki/Trainer-Backsprite-Editing)). Six front entries use double-size pics. Nothing sourced covers trainer face or outline conventions, so the style evidence is one sprite, the May example file, which looks like a genuine ROM extraction but is unverified as one. It is 64x64 with binary alpha, **15 opaque colors (exactly the limit)**, 810 opaque pixels (19.8% of the canvas, against 12-65% for Pokemon), one 8-connected component, and a bounding box of 37x55 with margins L9, R18, T5, B4 (measured). The figure is 86% of canvas height and about 3.4 heads tall (inference). Boundary pixels are 249 (30.7% of opaque): pure black #000000 is 39%, tinted dark (brown #7b5952, plum #4a3039, blue-gray #293842, gray-violet #636173) is 50%, and unoutlined fills are about 11%. Black is limited to gloves, shoes, ball and shorts, while skin and hair take tinted dark outlines (measured plus visual). The May file's pure black differs from the (16,16,16) in the sampled Pokemon palettes, and the notes do not resolve whether this is a trainer-versus-Pokemon difference, a modification, or a quirk like Gyarados. No dithering appears (0 checker fields, 4 isolated diagonal windows), shading is hard 2-3 tone steps with L* steps of 9-22, and both eyes are 2x2 (one dark and one white column, 5px apart, rows 14-15) with a 3px red mouth and no nose or brows (measured). The 13.8% orphan rate (112 pixels with no same-colored 8-neighbor) and 277 of 358 same-colour regions being 2px or smaller mean a "zero orphans" rule would wrongly reject the reference. This is one sample and gives no variance.

## Measuring whether a sprite reads: proposals calibrated on one sprite

No source gave numeric readability thresholds, and no published automatic pixel-art metric was found. Tutorials agree on principles: lightness contrast beats hue (test in grayscale), the silhouette must read as a solid fill, clusters beat isolated pixels, and avoid banding, doubles and irregular stair steps ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/learn/color-palettes/1), [PixNote](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/glossary), [Divoom](https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/fix-jaggies-pixel-art-without-blur)). The formulas below are the notes' proposals. Bracketed values are measured on the May sprite and are the only calibration. Notation: M is the opaque mask, L* is CIE L* from sRGB (D65), and boundary B is the set of pixels in M with a 4-neighbor outside M.

| Metric | Formula | May value | Proposed target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fill ratio | |M| / (bbox w*h) | 0.40 | none; Pokemon cover 12-65% of canvas (measured) |
| Min thickness | |M| - |opening(M, 2x2)| | 5 px (3x3: 27) | 2x2 near zero, small tips allowed |
| Connectivity | 8-connected components of M | 1 | 1 unless parts are intentionally floating |
| Orphan rate | pixels with no same-color 8-neighbor / |M| | 13.8% (4-neighbor 26.0%) | compare to references, never zero |
| Region fragmentation | 4-connected same-color regions; share at most 2px | 358; 77% | per region class, not global |
| Outline continuity | share of B with L* < 45 | 89% | about 85-90% for trainers (one sample); Pokemon edge near-black 21-75% (measured) |
| Adjacent contrast | share of color-change N4 pairs with dL < 10 | 11.4% (< 5: 1.3%, < 20: 34.1%) | flag semantically different parts with dL < 10 |
| Tone ladder | per-material tones and consecutive L* steps | 2-3 tones, steps 9-22 | 2-3 tones, step at least about 10 L* (trainer sample); Pokemon luma steps 25-45 (measured) |
| Eye | area; dL(dark eye, skin) | 2x2; large | at least 2x2; darkest at least about 40 L* below skin |
| Dither detector | ABBA 2x2 windows and checker fields | 4 windows, 0 fields | none for small sprites |
| Squint test | SSIM of L* or silhouette IoU after 2x/4x box downscale | not measured | unspecified, no source gives a number |

Metrics 11 (eye symmetry error: May's eyes differ in which dark pixel is darker, unverified as deliberate), 12 (mirror IoU: head 0.757, full figure 0.523, for frontal heads only), 14 (banding detector) and 15 (jaggy score from run-length ratios) are in the notes and unmeasured or lightly measured. The thin-stroke fraction (78%) does not discriminate for outlined sprites, so apply it to non-outline colors only. Because the numbers come from one trainer sprite, use them as flags rather than gates, and calibrate against pokeemerald Pokemon front PNGs.

## Rules for a conversion algorithm, in priority order

Targets are defaults taken from the notes' measurements, with ranges where the notes give them. Tags: **M** measured, **S** sourced, **V** visual, **U** unverified or inference.

| # | Rule | Default and range | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Output a 64x64 4-bit indexed PNG plus a 16-entry palette, with transparency at index 0 and binary alpha, no antialiasing | 15 visible colors max; target 10-15; wiki advises 14 | S, M |
| 2 | Quantize to 5 bits per channel (BGR555) | `c5 = c8>>3`; expand with `c5*255/31` | S, M |
| 3 | Fit the silhouette first: bottom-anchor, record the bounding box rounded to multiples of 8, and offset from the bottom | large species up to 64 wide, offset 0-3; small up to 12-20 | S |
| 4 | Keep one connected silhouette with 1-2px-wide minimum parts, merge small detail (fingers, toes, spikes into 2-3 nubs), fill 1px gaps unless defining | min width 2px, tips at least 3px long | V, U |
| 5 | Outline: 1px, default near-black (16,16,16), never (0,0,0) for Pokemon | near-black on 21-75% of edge; 35-60% typical, 55-75% for small compact designs | M, U |
| 6 | Switch the outline to the darkest ramp tone of the adjacent fill on light-facing edges, pale or white parts, flames and fins; reuse the ramp's shadow color to save slots | selout 25-79% of edge, same hue as fill only | M, V, U |
| 7 | Remove doubled L-corners from the outline ring | residual under about 3% of edge | M (crude) |
| 8 | Palette budget: outline 1, primary ramp 4-5, secondary 3-4, tertiary 3, spare 2-3 (eye, mouth, white, gray) | sum about 14-15 | M (8 species), U |
| 9 | Shade as flat 3-5 tone bands with upper-left light, highlight on the upper-left of each volume, shadow lower-right, darkest tone on right and down edges | light about (-0.5,-0.5,0.7); x stronger than y | M (crude), U |
| 10 | Ramp luma steps for 4 tones | about 200-240, 160-190, 105-130, 60-85; step 25-45; outline 16 | M, U |
| 11 | Hue shift per material, shadows toward red for warm, toward cyan-blue for green and blue, saturation +5 to +15 per step; zero shift allowed | 4-12 degrees per step; highlights toward yellow or white | M, U |
| 12 | Keep saturation high for strong-color characters, no extra boost for sRGB display | mid-tone S 60-100% | M, U |
| 13 | Spend pure white (#ffffff) only on 1-3 pixel clusters (eye shine, teeth, claws); broad white surfaces get a cool 3-4 tone off-white ramp | white 0.2-1.9% of pixels; ramp luma about 240/205/178/147, S 7-27, H 215-240 | M |
| 14 | Interior separation: color step if adjacent part fills differ by 2 or more ramp steps, otherwise insert a 1px line in the darkest ramp tone of the part behind or near-black | threshold about one ramp step | V (3-4 sprites), U |
| 15 | Eyes: dark contour around a 2x2 to 3x3 iris with one white shine pixel upper-left, plus a gray corner pixel at 3x3 and larger; mouth a 1px near-black line, with a 2-3px tongue or teeth block only if the source shows an open mouth; cheeks and emblems at least 3x3 with a highlight and a shade pixel | eye 2x2 to 4x3 | M (Pikachu), V |
| 16 | Dither sparingly: checker only at the boundary of two adjacent ramp steps, 1-2 px band, on regions over about 8x8; disable on small sprites and on edges | budget about 1-4% of opaque pixels; off below about 1100 opaque px | M, U |
| 17 | Fur uses jagged 1-2px steps plus a selout rim; flames use 3-4 step tapering ramps with no near-black outline | n/a | V |
| 18 | Do not enforce zero orphans: allow 1px highlights, flecks and eye pixels, and test orphan rate only against reference sprites | May 13.8%; boundary only: reject isolated outline pixels | M (one sprite) |
| 19 | Keep palette indices separable by region so a shiny palette swap works | recolorable regions get own indices | S, M |
| 20 | Validate against the proposed metrics as flags, not gates: outline continuity at least about 85% for trainers, semantic-part dL at least 10, eye at least 2x2, one component | see table above | U (single sample) |

## Conclusion

The central lesson is that Gen 3 legibility is selective rather than minimal. Detail survives because a few mechanisms do most of the work: a shared near-black outline that yields to a tinted dark ramp tone where a black ring would flatten a pale part, lightness-led ramps with 25-45 luma steps, and scarce pure white. High orphan and tiny-region counts (13.8% orphans and 77% regions of 2px or smaller in the May file) show the look is built from deliberate single pixels, so a tool that cleans toward zero orphans or a pure-black outline would move away from the target.

The caveats weigh as much as the findings. The samples are 8-16 hand-picked Pokemon and one trainer. The artist workflow, the Gen 2 and Gen 4 comparisons, FRLG-versus-RSE policy, and metal, stone and ghost-type handling are unsourced or unmeasured. The May file's pure-black #000000 against the Pokemon's (16,16,16) is unexplained. Next steps are a calibration run over pokeemerald and pokefirered front PNGs for outline share, dither share and orphan rates, and a calibration of the readability metrics on Red, Leaf, Brendan and gym leader sprites before any of the numeric targets are treated as gates.
